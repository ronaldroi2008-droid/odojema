If you earn $15 per hour and work 40 hours per week for 52 weeks per year, your annual salary is approximately **$31,200 before taxes**.

```
$15 × 40 hours × 52 weeks = $31,200
```

That works out to approximately:

- **$600 per week**
- **$2,600 per month**
- **$31,200 per year**

## How much is $15 an hour per year?

The formula for converting any hourly wage to a yearly salary is:

```
Hourly Rate × Hours Per Week × Weeks Per Year
```

For a full-time schedule at $15 an hour:

```
$15 × 40 × 52 = $31,200
```

A full-time job is normally counted as 2,080 hours per year (40 hours × 52 weeks). Multiply your hourly rate by 2,080 and you get your annual pay. This is the same method our guide on [how to convert hourly pay to annual salary](https://odojema.com/blog/how-to-convert-hourly-pay-to-annual-salary) uses for any wage.

Here is how the annual salary changes if you work fewer hours:

| Work Schedule | Annual Salary |
|---|---|
| 40 hrs/week | $31,200 |
| 35 hrs/week | $27,300 |
| 30 hrs/week | $23,400 |
| 20 hrs/week | $15,600 |

### What if you take unpaid time off?

The $31,200 figure assumes you are paid for all 52 weeks. If you are an hourly employee without paid vacation or holidays, every week you do not work is a week you do not get paid. Two unpaid weeks off would look like this:

```
$15 × 40 hours × 50 weeks = $30,000
```

That is $1,200 less than the full-year figure. If your job includes paid time off, paid holidays, or paid sick leave, those hours count toward your annual pay, so the $31,200 figure stands.

## How much is $15 an hour per month?

To convert to a monthly figure, divide the annual salary by 12:

```
Annual Salary:  $31,200
Monthly Salary: $31,200 ÷ 12 = $2,600
```

Here is how that breaks down for common pay schedules:

| Period | Income |
|---|---|
| Monthly | $2,600 |
| Twice monthly (24 paychecks) | $1,300 |
| Biweekly (26 paychecks) | $1,200 |
| Weekly | $600 |

Both twice monthly and biweekly are called "twice a month" in everyday speech, but they are different schedules. Twice monthly (semi-monthly) means two paychecks per month, usually on the 15th and the last day, for a total of 24 a year. Biweekly means one paycheck every two weeks, for a total of 26 a year.

If you are paid biweekly, two months each year will include three paychecks. In those months you receive $1,800 instead of $2,400, and many people use them to catch up on bills or build savings.

## How much is $15 an hour per week?

Weekly pay is the simplest calculation:

```
$15 × 40 = $600
```

You would earn **$600 per week before taxes** working a full 40 hours. Here is how weekly income changes with your hours:

| Hours Worked | Weekly Income |
|---|---|
| 20 | $300 |
| 30 | $450 |
| 40 | $600 |
| 50 | $750 |

The 50-hour row assumes every hour is paid at your regular rate. Many hourly workers in the United States are entitled to overtime pay for hours beyond 40 in a week, which we cover below. At $15 an hour, an 8-hour day is $120.

## How much is $15 an hour every two weeks?

For a biweekly paycheck, double the weekly figure:

```
$600 × 2 = $1,200
```

Your approximate biweekly paycheck is **$1,200 before taxes**. You can also get this by dividing the annual salary by 26: $31,200 ÷ 26 = $1,200. Your actual deposit will be lower once taxes and other deductions come out, which is what the next section covers.

## How much is $15 an hour after taxes?

Your take-home pay depends on several things:

- **State:** some states have no income tax, while others take several percent
- **Filing status:** single, married, or head of household
- **Deductions:** such as retirement contributions and health insurance premiums
- **Benefits:** employer-sponsored plans can reduce or increase your net pay

Because these vary, the figures below are estimates. They start with a simple federal scenario: a single filer with no dependents, taking the 2026 standard deduction of $16,100, with no other income.

```
Gross annual income:               $31,200
Standard deduction:               -$16,100
Taxable income:                    $15,100

Federal income tax:
  10% on the first $12,400         $1,240
  12% on the remaining $2,700      $324
  Total federal income tax         $1,564

Payroll taxes (FICA):
  Social Security (6.2%)           $1,934.40
  Medicare (1.45%)                 $452.40
  Total FICA                       $2,386.80

Take-home before state tax:        about $27,249 per year
```

That is about **$2,271 per month** or about **$1,048 per biweekly paycheck** before any state tax or other deductions.

### Take-home pay at $15 an hour by state

State income tax is the biggest difference between workers earning the same wage. Here are three examples, all using the same federal numbers as above:

| Scenario | State income tax | Take-home per year | Per month | Per biweekly paycheck |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No state income tax (for example Texas, Florida) | $0 | about $27,249 | about $2,271 | about $1,048 |
| Pennsylvania (3.07% flat tax on wages) | about $958 | about $26,291 | about $2,191 | about $1,011 |
| Example state with a 5% tax on wages | $1,560 | about $25,689 | about $2,141 | about $988 |

The Pennsylvania row uses the state's flat 3.07% rate with no standard deduction. The 5% row is only an illustration, because many states have their own deductions, brackets, and credits. Some cities and counties add a local income tax on top. Health insurance premiums, retirement contributions, and other deductions will lower these numbers further.

A realistic range for a full-time worker earning $15 an hour is **$2,000 to $2,300 per month** after taxes and common deductions. The higher end applies to workers in states without income tax and with few payroll deductions. The lower end applies to workers in states with income tax, or those who pay for health insurance through their paycheck.

For a precise number, check your state's tax rules and your employer's deduction schedule, or use the [IRS Tax Withholding Estimator](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/tax-withholding-estimator). Tax brackets and deductions change each year, so confirm the current figures when you plan.

## What about overtime at $15 an hour?

If you are a non-exempt employee, federal law generally requires overtime pay of at least one and a half times your regular rate for hours worked beyond 40 in a week. The [U.S. Department of Labor overtime page](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/overtime) explains who is covered. At $15 an hour, the overtime rate is:

```
$15 × 1.5 = $22.50 per hour
```

Here is what a 45-hour week looks like:

```
40 regular hours × $15  = $600.00
5 overtime hours × $22.50 = $112.50
Total weekly pay          = $712.50
```

If you worked 45 hours every week for a year, your annual pay would be $712.50 × 52 = $37,050, nearly $6,000 more than a standard full-time schedule. Overtime rules can vary by state and job type, and some salaried roles are exempt. For more detail, see our guides on [how to calculate overtime pay](https://odojema.com/blog/how-to-calculate-overtime-pay) and [overtime pay rates](https://odojema.com/blog/overtime-pay-rates-explained), or try the [overtime pay calculator](https://odojema.com/overtime-pay-calculator).

## Is $15 an hour a good salary?

Whether $15 an hour is "good" depends on where you live and what you need it to cover. Four things matter most:

- **Cost of living:** housing, food, and transportation costs vary widely
- **Location:** a wage that is comfortable in a small town may be tight in a big city
- **Household size:** supporting dependents changes the math significantly
- **Debt obligations:** student loans, car payments, and credit cards reduce what is left

### Pros of earning $15 an hour

- It is well above the federal minimum wage of $7.25 per hour.
- It is a common starting wage for many entry-level and service jobs.
- Full-time hours give you predictable, steady income.

### Cons of earning $15 an hour

- It can be difficult to cover expenses in high-cost cities.
- Savings potential is limited in expensive areas.
- It leaves little cushion for emergencies if you have dependents or debt.

### A sample budget

Using the estimated take-home of about $2,270 per month (no state income tax), a basic 50/30/20 budget looks like this:

```
Needs (50%):    $1,135
Wants (30%):    $681
Savings (20%):  $454
```

A common guideline is to keep rent to about 30% of your income. That would be roughly $681 per month based on take-home pay, or $780 based on gross pay. In many markets that is not enough for an apartment on your own, which is why many people at this wage share housing costs or live with family. In lower-cost areas the same budget goes much further.

## Hourly pay vs. salary

If you are comparing an hourly job at $15 an hour with a salaried job, convert both to the same basis first. An hourly job at $15 an hour full-time is $31,200 a year. A salaried job at $31,200 may look identical, but the details differ:

- **Overtime:** hourly employees are often eligible for overtime pay, while many salaried employees are not.
- **Unpaid time:** hourly workers may not be paid for holidays or sick days unless the employer offers paid leave.
- **Benefits:** salaried positions sometimes include more generous benefits.
- **Hours:** salaried roles may require more than 40 hours without extra pay.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is $15 an hour full-time?**
Yes, if you work 40 hours per week. At 40 hours per week, $15 an hour equals approximately $31,200 per year before taxes.

**How much is $15 an hour monthly?**
About $2,600 per month before taxes. After federal taxes and payroll taxes, take-home pay is roughly $2,000 to $2,300 per month, depending on your state and deductions.

**How much is $15 an hour biweekly?**
Approximately $1,200 every two weeks before taxes, based on 80 hours per paycheck.

**How much is $15 an hour per week?**
$600 per week for a 40-hour week, before taxes.

**Is $15 an hour above minimum wage?**
In many locations, yes. The federal minimum wage is $7.25 per hour, but minimum wage laws vary by state and city, and some states and cities already set a minimum above $15 an hour. Check the current minimum wage where you work on the [U.S. Department of Labor minimum wage page](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/minimum-wage).

**How much is $15 an hour after taxes?**
For a single filer working full-time, take-home pay is typically about $24,000 to $28,000 per year, or roughly $2,000 to $2,300 per month. The exact amount depends on your state, filing status, and deductions.

**Can you live on $15 an hour?**
It depends on local living costs, housing expenses, and household size. In lower-cost areas, a single person can often manage on $15 an hour. In high-cost cities, or with dependents, it is much harder without sharing expenses or earning additional income.
