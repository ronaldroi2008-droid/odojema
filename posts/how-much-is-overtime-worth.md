Working overtime can significantly increase your income, but many employees wonder whether the extra hours are actually worth it. The answer depends on:

- Your hourly wage
- Your overtime rate
- The number of overtime hours worked
- Whether you receive time and a half or double time

In this guide, you will learn how much overtime is worth, how to calculate overtime earnings, and see real-world examples using common hourly wages.

## Quick answer

Overtime is worth more than your regular hourly wage because employers often pay a premium rate for overtime hours. Common overtime rates include:

| Pay Type | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Regular pay | 1× |
| Time and a half | 1.5× |
| Double time | 2× |

**Example:** if you earn $20 per hour, then:

```
Regular Pay      = $20/hour
Time and a Half  = $30/hour
Double Time      = $40/hour
```

Working 10 overtime hours at time and a half would earn:

```
$30 × 10 = $300
```

## Key takeaways

- Overtime is usually paid at a higher rate than your regular wage.
- Time and a half pays 150% of your regular hourly rate.
- Double time pays 200% of your regular hourly rate.
- Overtime earnings = overtime rate × overtime hours.
- The higher your hourly wage, the more each overtime hour is worth.

## How to calculate overtime worth

Use this formula:

```
Overtime Earnings = Overtime Rate × Overtime Hours
```

Where:

```
Overtime Rate = Hourly Rate × Overtime Multiplier
```

### Example: time and a half overtime

Assume an hourly rate of $20 and 8 overtime hours.

```
Step 1: $20 × 1.5 = $30
Step 2: $30 × 8   = $240
```

Total overtime earnings: **$240**.

### Example: double time overtime

Assume an hourly rate of $20 and 8 overtime hours.

```
Step 1: $20 × 2 = $40
Step 2: $40 × 8 = $320
```

Total overtime earnings: **$320**.

## Overtime vs regular pay

Consider an employee earning $25 per hour who works 40 regular hours and 10 overtime hours at time and a half.

**Regular pay only:**

```
40 × $25 = $1,000
```

**With overtime:**

```
Regular pay:    $1,000
Overtime:       10 × $37.50 = $375
Total weekly:   $1,375
```

The difference is **$375 in additional income** for the week. If that happened every week for a full year, it would add up to $375 × 52 = $19,500.

## When is overtime most valuable?

Overtime tends to be most valuable when:

- Your hourly wage is higher
- Double time rates apply
- Holiday pay is available
- You work a large number of overtime hours

Employees earning higher hourly wages often see the greatest benefit from overtime premiums, because the same multiplier is applied to a larger base rate.

## Common mistakes when calculating overtime

### Using the wrong multiplier

Time and a half is 1.5×. Double time is 2×. Mixing them up changes your total significantly.

### Forgetting total hours

Many employees calculate the overtime rate correctly but forget to multiply it by the number of overtime hours worked.

### Ignoring employer policies

Some employers have unique overtime rules that may differ from standard calculations. Always check your contract and local labor regulations.
