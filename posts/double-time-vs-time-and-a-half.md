If you have ever worked overtime, you have probably heard the terms time and a half and double time. Both increase your earnings above your regular hourly wage, but they are not the same.

The biggest difference is simple:

- **Time and a half** pays 1.5× your regular rate.
- **Double time** pays 2× your regular rate.

Understanding the difference helps you estimate overtime earnings, compare job offers, and verify your paycheck. In this guide, you will learn:

- What time and a half means
- What double time means
- How each is calculated
- Real-world examples
- Which one pays more

## Quick answer

| Pay Type | Multiplier | Example ($20/hour) |
|---|---|---|
| Regular pay | 1× | $20/hour |
| Time and a half | 1.5× | $30/hour |
| Double time | 2× | $40/hour |

**Formula:**

```
Time and a Half = Hourly Rate × 1.5
Double Time     = Hourly Rate × 2
```

If you earn $20 per hour:

- Time and a half = **$30 per hour**
- Double time = **$40 per hour**

Double time pays significantly more because it doubles your regular wage instead of adding only 50%.

## Key takeaways

- Time and a half pays 150% of your regular hourly rate.
- Double time pays 200% of your regular hourly rate.
- Double time always pays more than time and a half.
- Most overtime is paid at time and a half.
- Double time usually applies only in special situations.

## What is time and a half?

Time and a half means an employee earns this for eligible overtime hours:

```
Regular Hourly Rate × 1.5
```

For an hourly rate of $20:

```
$20 × 1.5 = $30
```

The overtime rate becomes **$30 per hour**. For a complete explanation, see [what is time and a half pay](https://odojema.com/blog/what-is-time-and-a-half-pay).

## What is double time?

Double time means an employee earns this for qualifying hours:

```
Regular Hourly Rate × 2
```

For an hourly rate of $20:

```
$20 × 2 = $40
```

The overtime rate becomes **$40 per hour**. Double time is generally reserved for specific situations, such as:

- Holidays
- Extended shifts
- Union agreements
- Employer policies
- Certain state labor laws

## Which pays more?

Double time always pays more than time and a half. Using a $25 hourly wage:

| Pay Type | Earnings Per Hour |
|---|---|
| Regular pay | $25 |
| Time and a half | $37.50 |
| Double time | $50 |

```
$50 − $37.50 = $12.50 more per hour
```

Over a long shift, that difference adds up quickly.

### Example: 8 overtime hours

Assume an hourly rate of $20 and 8 overtime hours.

**Time and a half:**

```
$20 × 1.5 = $30 per hour
$30 × 8   = $240
```

**Double time:**

```
$20 × 2 = $40 per hour
$40 × 8 = $320
```

**Difference:**

```
$320 − $240 = $80
```

Double time earns **$80 more** for the same number of hours.

## When do employers pay double time?

The exact rules depend on:

- Country
- State or province
- Labor laws
- Union contracts
- Employer policies

Common situations include:

- Certain holidays
- Very long shifts
- Consecutive workdays
- Special overtime agreements

Not every employer offers double time. In the United States, federal law does not require double time, so it usually comes from state law, a union contract, or company policy. Check your local rules and employment agreement to see what applies to you.

## Common misconceptions

### Double time is not 150%

Some workers mistakenly think double time is the same as time and a half. It is not:

```
Time and a Half = 150% of your regular rate
Double Time     = 200% of your regular rate
```

### Not all overtime is double time

Most overtime is paid at time and a half. Double time is less common and applies only in specific situations.

### Rules vary by location

Overtime laws differ between countries, states, and employers. Always verify local labor regulations.
